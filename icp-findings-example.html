How we mine the call vault

What we did

Bloom scored the client’s live call archive, not a stated ICP slide. Evidence came from recorded sales and delivery meetings, plus the language already sitting in email threads. The filter was best-fit accounts (retention, recency, and whether the buyer matched the work), not whoever had the prettiest case study.

The vault covered about 3,347 meetings across the live archive. Pattern files from research were used as a second pass so a single loud call could not set the ICP.

HubSpot and Salesforce show up as stack categories in the quotes below. Named companies, people, and commercial figures are stripped.

Verbatim from the call vault

What buyers actually say

This is the emotional core. Language is verbatim or near-verbatim from the vault. Each block: what they say, then what it feels like.

Inherited machine nobody understands

“The person who built it is gone. The workflows still fire. Nobody knows why.”
“Overly complicated, somewhat black-box custom solution. Nobody really understood the logic.”
“Single point of failure.” / “House of cards.”

Fear of looking incompetent a few weeks in. Urgency without bandwidth. They want an outside forcing function, not another hire who will get swallowed by tickets.

Marketing cannot prove what is working

“I can’t with any confidence tell you where leads are coming from.”
“Marketing can’t prove ROI to the CEO.”
“Attribution … swallowed by a Direct bucket.”
“Only X% of leads … with correct lead sourcing.”
“Hours of manual forensic work each week.”

The team is already working hard. Leadership keeps asking for proof. The reflex is “make more content,” which makes the shame worse. Post-migration orphan attribution (HubSpot or Salesforce after a move) is a repeating cause, not a one-off ticket.

Flying the sales org blind

“Zero ability to forecast. Zero ability to track sales activity.”
“Performing a pipeline review with X-day-old data, and nobody knows. There’s no baseline.”
“Sales moved off [CRM] onto Excel.”
“HubSpot for X months … essentially no value.”

Anger at the IT backlog, plus quiet dread before forecast calls. They have already stopped waiting.

The system and reality do not match

“The data doesn’t match what the team is experiencing.”
“Shadow spreadsheet.”
“We know it’s wrong, but we don’t know how wrong.”

Learned helplessness. The CRM became a tax. The real source of truth left the building. Dual-CRM sync debt (picklist mismatch, records that will not stick) shows up as delivery pain, not a footnote.

I believe it. I cannot sell it upstairs

“Didn’t come away with a clear understanding of what they’d be getting.”
“He left skeptical enough to say he’d call cheaper, more experienced alternatives.”
“He remains open… but won’t push it to the decision maker without stronger justification.”
“Adding headcount without fixing top-of-funnel infrastructure will repeat burning through reps.”
“Board approval is the blocker, not internal alignment.”

The champion is bought. The CEO, CFO, or board was not in the room. Without ammo, the deal dies politely. “Don’t hire another seller until the funnel exists” is the upstairs sentence that keeps landing.

Who to hire the team against

Who they actually are

Self-research personas (site and search) are the first two. Referral and deal-support is the third. Channel work on LinkedIn is the fourth. An emerging fifth is a board motion, not a search bet.

Hottest live champion is still the new in-seat leader, now including Head of Sales / SVP Sales. Static title lists that start at CFO / COO miss the two titles that dominate the vault.

Where they research vs where they decide

Research vs decision

Generic path: problem identification, solution exploration, engage and discover, supplier selection, then experience. Website-class touchpoints are search, site content, and case studies they can forward. Revenue executives rarely self-research. They arrive by referral and events.

PersonaAwarenessConsiderationDecision
New in-seat GTM leader Organic search. RevOps communities and webinars. Non-paid LinkedIn. Referrals. Website content and case studies. Discovery call. Audit / roadmap as an executive summary. Proposal they can send up.
Overwhelmed operator RevOps communities. Technical non-paid LinkedIn. Referrals. Technical site content and case studies. Diagnostic call. Roadmap packaged as exec-ready materials. The deal converts with a sponsor above them.
Revenue exec Referrals and events. Almost no self-search. Warm intro. A simple case they believe. Confidence in the plan and the team before they commit.
PE operating partner Relationships, LinkedIn, peer forums. Cold outreach can work here. Portfolio playbook. Repeatability across companies. Intro to the portco champion. The operating partner rarely signs the contract.

Content that earns the meeting opens on inheritance, black boxes, Direct bucket, and “prove it to the CEO.” Decision-stage sales assets do not belong as blog posts.

Questions before they say yes

Questions before yes

These are the questions that stall a yes even when the champion likes the work. A findings report answers them in buyer language, then gives the champion a page they can forward.

  • Does this partner help me build the internal case, in exec language, for the cost of broken attribution?
  • Who signs off above me, and do I have materials they will actually read?
  • Will you fix the funnel before we hire another seller, or are we repeating the same burn?
  • After a HubSpot or Salesforce migration, can you find the orphaned Direct-bucket leads, or are we guessing?
  • Can you show up as the third party who makes stakeholders appear, not another ticket-taker?
  • If the person who understood the workflows is gone, how fast do we get a system we can trust?

What the starter team works first

Short roadmap

Customer Intelligence works this first. Production and sales teams inherit the same vault.

  1. Keep site and search copy on new in-seat GTM leaders and overwhelmed operators. Generic “RevOps education” does not match vault volume.
  2. Put inherited-CRM, first weeks in seat, Direct bucket, and “don’t hire another seller until the funnel exists” on the pages that have to close upstairs.
  3. Treat post-migration orphan attribution as a named problem on choose-partner and integration content.
  4. Give the champion exec-summary ammo. Feature menus do not survive a CEO review.
  5. Do not bet organic search on the revenue-exec persona. Support that path with proof they can be handed in a referral.
  6. PE portfolio motion lives on LinkedIn and a repeatable playbook, not a long-tail keyword pile.
