On the always-on team

You own the Mac mini. Bloom installs it in your office. Day to day you talk to the Orchestrator in Slack. A human from Bloom stays. This seat is one employee on that roster, not a project that ends at kickoff.

What this seat does

Owns the competitive set against the buyers you actually sell into, not a generic list. Public sources only. Every claim dated.

  • The names that show up in your deals.
  • What changed on their site this month, dated.
  • Where you can claim ground in buyer language.

The monthly report

Competitive position report. Delivered day 1.

  1. Executive synthesis: what changed in the set this month and what it means
  2. The competitor set and why these names, tied to who actually shows up in deals
  3. Positioning matrix: their claim, the client's claim, what buyers actually say they want
  4. Message comparison: where the client is differentiated, identical, or behind
  5. Content and keyword overlap: terms and topics competitors hold that map to the client's buyers
  6. Packaging and pricing signals where publicly stated
  7. Review mining: what customers say about competitors, and the recurring complaints the client could answer
  8. Change log: what each competitor published, changed, or launched this month
  9. Where you are beatable and where you can claim ground
  10. Decisions and next actions

Between reports: Slack alerts only when a change is material. Comparison-page tickets and battlecard notes go to Content and Deal Enablement.
