Content Director

On the always-on team

You own the Mac mini. Bloom installs it in your office. Day to day you talk to the Orchestrator in Slack. A human from Bloom stays. This seat is one employee on that roster, not a project that ends at kickoff.

What this seat does

Owns what exists on the site, what should be fixed, and what should be built next. Audit first. Rewrites and interview-sourced pages. Net-new volume is not the flagship.

  • Pages scored for keep, rework, replace, or retire.
  • Rewrites grounded in people who actually know the work.
  • Staging preview. Human approval. Then publish. Nothing auto-publishes.

The monthly report

Content performance and audit report. Delivered day 1. The audit is re-run monthly and compared with last month, so closed gaps drop off and new ones surface.

  1. The short version: overall verdict, strongest asset, largest trust or conversion risk, highest-leverage next move
  2. What we audited: how many pages, what was deep-scored, and what was deliberately left out
  3. Scoring framework: buyer alignment, search and answer readiness, voice, accuracy and trust, conversion utility
  4. Verdicts at a glance: keep, rework, replace, or retire, by content layer
  5. Findings, each with evidence, why it matters, recommendation, and priority
  6. Buyer language alignment: what buyers say, what the site currently says, recommended language, pages affected
  7. Critical gaps: missing asset, evidence of demand, buyer, format, priority
  8. Refinements to existing content: URL, verdict, specific change, expected effect, effort
  9. Accuracy and trust repairs: claim, location, evidence status, repair
  10. What shipped last month and how it performed
  11. 90-day content repair plan and decisions table

Between reports: next week's topics and rewrites proposed on Friday, drafting after a yes, SME review where the topic needs it, then publish. Small fixes and decay alerts land in Slack.
