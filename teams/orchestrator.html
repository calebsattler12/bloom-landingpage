Orchestrator

The person you talk to in Slack. Outlines future agent strategy: which seats to stand up next, and what they should work. Keeps the week visible so the rest of the employees do not go quiet.

On the always-on team

You own the Mac mini. Bloom installs it in your office. Day to day you talk to the Orchestrator in Slack. A human from Bloom stays. This seat is one employee on that roster, not a project that ends at kickoff.

What this seat does

One surface in Slack. Compiles the month. Names how the roster grows. A human from Bloom still reviews what reaches you.

  • Outlines future agent strategy from the live work: which seats come next, in what order, and why.
  • Takes today's ask and sends it to the right employee. You do not have to know who owns what.
  • Keeps the week visible so work does not vanish into a tool nobody opened.

The monthly package

One package on day 1, plus a short weekly pulse between deliveries. The point is one read.

  1. Cover synthesis: the three things that matter most this month, and what needs a decision
  2. The consolidated decisions table: recommended actions in one place, ranked, with owner and due date
  3. Each seat's report, attached in full
  4. Progress against the 90-day roadmap: what shipped, what slipped, what changed
  5. What the team produced and what is waiting on a yes
  6. Next month's plan and any proposed roadmap changes
  7. Weekly pulse between reports: a short digest of what ran that week

Between packages: batched approvals with a recommendation on each, plain-English intake in Slack, and a note when the 90-day plan should be re-scoped rather than treated as fiction.
